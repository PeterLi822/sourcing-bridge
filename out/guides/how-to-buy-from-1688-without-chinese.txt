3:T485,1688.com is the domestic wholesale marketplace of Alibaba Group — the place where Chinese retailers and resellers buy their inventory. Think of it this way: Alibaba.com is the international, English-language storefront with export-ready suppliers; AliExpress is the retail counter where anyone can buy single units at a markup; 1688 is the back room where the actual wholesale happens, entirely in Chinese, priced in yuan, built for domestic buyers. That is why prices on 1688 are usually the lowest you will find anywhere — you are buying at the same counter as the Chinese sellers who later mark the goods up for export. The trade-off: no English interface, suppliers who assume you read Chinese and pay through Alipay, domestic-only shipping, and minimum order quantities set for businesses, not individuals. It is not a scam site and it is not a secret — it is simply a market that was never designed for you. The good news: with browser translation and the right workflow, a non-Chinese speaker can research products, vet suppliers, and even place orders — or skip the hard parts entirely by using an agent, which we cover in the final section.4:T42e,Before you touch 1688, set up translation. In Chrome, right-click any page and choose Translate to English — the whole interface becomes readable in one click. For searching, do not type English keywords into the 1688 search bar and hope; open a translation tool, type your product keyword in English (say, dog nail grinder), copy the Chinese result, and paste that into the 1688 search box. Save the translations that work in a simple glossary document — you will reuse them constantly. On your phone, a camera-translation app lets you point at packaging, labels, and supplier chat screenshots. Two practical notes: machine translation mangles product names (expect gems like explosive press type concealer), so read for meaning, not poetry; and when messaging suppliers, keep your sentences short and simple — Can I order 50 pieces first? translates far more reliably than a paragraph of conditions. Translation gets you 90 percent of the way there. The remaining 10 percent — payment, domestic shipping, negotiation nuance — is where agents earn their keep.5:T46f,You cannot visit the factory, so build quality control into the process. Step one is always samples: order one to three units and inspect them against the listing — materials, function, finish, packaging, and whether the thing actually does what the photos promise. Test electronics through a full charge cycle; wash fabric items once; stress-test anything with a moving part. For larger orders, you have two options: ask your agent to do a pre-shipment check (most agents will photograph or video your goods before they ship — ask for this explicitly), or hire a third-party inspection service for orders large enough to justify the cost. Accept a basic truth: some defect rate is normal in low-cost manufacturing. What separates professionals from gamblers is having the defective-replacement policy agreed before the order, not argued about after. Document everything in the chat — translated chat logs are your contract. When the sample arrives, compare it against the listing photos side by side — differences in color, material, or finish at the sample stage predict exactly what your customers will complain about later.6:T42c,Here is the shortcut most non-Chinese-speaking dropshippers actually use: a sourcing agent. An agent is a company that buys from 1688 on your behalf — in yuan, through domestic payment, from vetted suppliers — then quality-checks the goods, stores them, and ships individual orders to your customers worldwide, usually with no per-order MOQ. CJ Dropshipping is one such agent operating in this space, alongside several others; compare a few on pricing, shipping lines, and support responsiveness before committing. This site's freight data, for example, comes from real CJ shipping quotes, which is why the numbers here reflect what you would actually pay rather than a supplier's optimistic estimate.
